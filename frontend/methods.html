How every layer, number and model in the app is produced, how it was checked, what it cannot tell you, and the open data it comes from. Everything below is reproducible from the repository.

Provenance: what each label means

Every metric and layer carries one of these labels, in the app and on this page.

real
Recorded by a sensor or published by an agency, shown as it is (drone video, camera pose, Sentinel-2 images).
derived
Computed from real data by a fixed, documented method (when each place burned, rate of spread, events).
external
Another organisation's product, used as published (EMS perimeters, FIRMS detections, ERA5 weather).
model
Output of a trained or physical model, with its measured accuracy (segmentation, spread scenario, visual search).
simulated
An illustrative exercise around the real incident (extra drones, aircraft, crews). Never an observation, never a forecast.

Larouco wildfire Ourense, Spain · 13–27 Aug 2025 · 33,132 ha

The default incident: a two-week wildfire reconstructed hour by hour from open satellite data, on a 30 m grid of 42 × 41 km (ETRS89 / UTM 29N). Pipeline: pipeline/larouco/.

When each place burned derived

  1. First detection. Each 30 m cell takes the time of the first NASA FIRMS detection whose footprint covers it: VIIRS S-NPP (375 m) and MODIS Terra/Aqua (1 km), 1,914 detections on 159 passes.
  2. Bounded by the perimeters. The twelve dated Copernicus EMS delineations (EMSR837) give a window for every cell: burned before an image that shows it burnt, after one that does not. A detection outside its window is moved to the window's middle.
  3. Between passes. EUMETSAT Meteosat fire pixels (LSA SAF FRP-PIXEL, every 15 min, ~3–4 km) re-time cells inside the gaps between polar passes.
  4. Gaps. Cells inside the final scar with no detection are interpolated (inverse-distance), and the field is smoothed (σ 150 m).

Validation: each intermediate EMS perimeter is held out in turn and predicted from everything else.

The method's parameters were chosen among 18 combinations on these same scores, so they are slightly optimistic. Meteosat does not change them: the perimeters are all taken around 11:00 UTC and cannot score the hour.

Derived from it

  • Burned area and expansion rate derived: hourly frames; the expansion rate is the growth of the equivalent radius √(area/π) over 6 h, the speed of the scar as a whole, lower than the head-fire speed.
  • Active front derived: cells that burned in the last 3 h. Satellite passes are hours apart, so no instantaneous front line is drawn.
  • Events derived: 19, computed from the reconstruction, the perimeters, FIRMS and ERA5 (fast runs, area milestones, driest hour, strongest gusts, each new perimeter). All need human review.
  • Fire power external: Meteosat FRP every 15 min, peak 14 GW on 16 Aug at 19:00 UTC; the most intense polar pass was 33 GW (MODIS, 17 Aug).

Sentinel-2 on the day real

The true-colour L2A product (TCI) of every pass from 11 to 26 Aug (11, 13, 16, 21, 23, 26 Aug, ~11:30 UTC), read from its 20 m overview and warped to the same lon/lat box as the basemap. The layer shows the latest pass before the timeline cursor. Tile seams are the product's own (per-tile atmospheric correction).

Spread scenario (hindcast) model

Least travel time on 90 m cells, re-evaluated every hour: a base rate R0 times fuel (ESA WorldCover), wind (ERA5), slope (IGN MDT25) and dryness (ERA5 vapour-pressure deficit). R0 = 34.3 m/h was calibrated only on the 16 Aug perimeter (5,219 ha); the model then ran freely. No suppression, no spotting.

Terrain, imagery and weather

  • Terrain real: IGN/CNIG MDT25 over the scar; AWS Terrain Tiles for the horizon. Packaged with the incident, no tile server. Shown with 1.8× vertical exaggeration.
  • Imagery real: IGN PNOA orthophoto (≈10 m, ≈7 m over the scar); Sentinel-2 cloudless 2016 by EOX for the horizon, colour-matched to PNOA. The real sun and night follow the timeline.
  • Vegetation real: tree and shrub cover from ESA WorldCover 10 m, drawn as instanced trees that char when the fire reaches them (stylised).
  • Weather external: ERA5 reanalysis via Open-Meteo, hourly, at the scar's centre (~25 km grid). Not an on-site sensor.

Limits

  • Timing between satellite passes is interpolated, and the first detection is not the ignition.
  • Smoke columns, spot fires and the instantaneous front are not measured.
  • The app has no data on people, houses or the real operation; the fleet around it is an exercise.

Pinhão drone burn Torre do Pinhão, Portugal · 1 Mar 2019 · 15 min

A prescribed burn filmed by a hovering DJI Phantom 4 Pro (BurnedAreaUAV v1.1, Zenodo 7944963), with hand-drawn burned-area masks every 4 s. Pipeline: pipeline/pinhao/.

Burned area, flames, smoke model

One frame every 4 s. Burned ground comes from a small neural network, LR-ASPP with a MobileNetV3-Large backbone (3.2 M parameters, torchvision, pretrained on COCO), fine-tuned on the dataset's masks at 512 × 288, with a majority vote over three frames. Flames and smoke come from colour rules (saturated orange-yellow; bright, grey, untextured); the dataset has no masks for them, and RGB cannot measure temperature.

Split by time, so no test frame is near a training frame: training 0–17,900 (180 frames), validation 18,000–22,500 (46), the dataset's own test split 20,250–22,450 (23).

From the image to the ground derived

A pinhole camera at the drone's pose from the DJI XMP metadata (24.9 m above the take-off point, gimbal yaw −46.7°, pitch −28.9°, calibrated focal length); every outline pixel is cast as a ray onto the elevation model (AWS Terrain Tiles), which turns masks into map polygons in m². Rows above 21% of the frame (the far field) are excluded.

Error in metres: not measured. Matching the frames against the DGT Ortos 2018 orthophoto (25 cm), automatically (SIFT + RANSAC, both orthorectified and rendered into the camera view) and by hand, found nothing sharp in both: an oblique 25 m view, smoke, a year of vegetation change. Bearings to the far track agree within about 2°, but the camera model puts the far field at about half its distance, so the pose's pitch or height likely differs during the video. No RMSE is claimed.

Derived from it

  • Rate of spread derived: the leading edge of the burned outline against one minute earlier (90th percentile of the displacement), m/min; the visible front only.
  • Events derived: first flames, fastest growth, flames outside the burned area, dense smoke, peak flame extent; all flagged for review.
  • Weather external: ERA5 via Open-Meteo for the site and hour.
  • Drones FT-02…06, aircraft, crews, large-fire scenario simulated: a deterministic exercise on the real terrain, tagged SIM.

AI in the browser

Optional, and on the visitor's device: nothing is uploaded. Each tool says what it downloads, from this site, and is remembered on that device once turned on. The incident analyst (Ask) is rule-based: no language model, nothing generated.

Visual search model

MobileCLIP-S0 (Apple). The image vectors are computed offline (frontend/scripts/ai/embed.mjs): for Pinhão, one frame every 2 s, whole and in three squares; for Larouco, the six Sentinel-2 passes cut into 2.7 km and 5.4 km squares. In the browser only the text encoder runs (about 50 MB once, int8, ONNX Runtime WebAssembly), ~0.1 s per query. Spanish queries are translated with a small wildfire vocabulary, and the English query searched is shown.

CLIP similarities are relative, and a fire scene pulls every fire-related word up. So the match strength also asks whether, in its best image, the query beats the ordinary contents of that kind of scene (flames, smoke, grass… / forest, clouds, farmland…). Calibrated on 58 queries for things that are and are not in the imagery (scripts/ai/calibrate.mjs): all on the right side. People, vehicles and aircraft are flagged as too small to see from orbit.

Live analysis model

The Pinhão segmenter (13 MB) runs on the playing video in a worker, with WebGPU when available (≈40 fps on a laptop) or WebAssembly; flames and smoke use the same colour rules. Its burned mask agrees with the recorded pipeline at IoU 0.98 over 22 frames (scripts/audit/live.mjs). It also runs on a video file of your own, which never leaves the browser; trained on one flight, it is a rough guide on other footage.

Reproduce

The app is a static site: every number above is in a precomputed incident package, rebuilt by the pipelines below (Python 3.13, pipeline/requirements.txt; Node 22 for the site).

# Larouco (no accounts needed, except Meteosat: a free EUMETSAT LSA SAF account)
python -m pipeline.larouco.download          # FIRMS, EMS, Sentinel-2, ERA5
python -m pipeline.larouco.meteosat download # optional, then: process
python -m pipeline.larouco.progression       # when each place burned + validation
python -m pipeline.larouco.severity          # dNBR
python -m pipeline.larouco.scenario          # spread hindcast
python -m pipeline.larouco.basemap           # packaged terrain and imagery
python -m pipeline.larouco.s2series          # Sentinel-2 on each pass
python -m pipeline.larouco.package           # → data/processed/larouco/

# Pinhão (BurnedAreaUAV from Zenodo 7944963)
python -m pipeline.pinhao.segmenter train    # fine-tune and evaluate the segmenter
python -m pipeline.pinhao.replay             # masks → ground → timeline, events, eval
python -m pipeline.pinhao.frames             # frames for visual search

# the site
cd frontend && node scripts/ai/embed.mjs && npm run build

Checks: python -m pytest pipeline/tests, scripts/e2e.mjs (87 functional checks), scripts/present-test.mjs (both tours), scripts/audit/devices.mjs and states.mjs (8 phones and tablets, 5 desktop sizes), ai.mjs, live.mjs.
